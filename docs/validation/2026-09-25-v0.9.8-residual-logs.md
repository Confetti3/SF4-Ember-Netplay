# v0.9.8 residual reports and the Linux log archive (2026-09-25)

Four reports reached the Ember Discord after v0.9.8, with one log archive from a Linux (Wine) player: `sf4e-logs-098-residual.zip`, 65 files, the game log `sf4e.log` and its nine rotations, the launcher logs, the sidecar bootstrap log and one helper status log per game process. The v0.9.8 build is `56e26d9f`; the older rotations are rc builds (`b202d20d`, `fe39b0f0`, `a790da53`, `4f7aadf3`) from 22 and 23 September. Everything below was read from those files; nothing was reproduced on a machine with the game.

The fixes and the code review are in [2026-09-25-residual-logs-and-code-quality](../reviews/2026-09-25-residual-logs-and-code-quality.md). Finding IDs continue the ledger in [2026-09-23-ledger-triage](../reviews/2026-09-23-ledger-triage.md).

## F-008: closed

The room no longer closes on `match_teardown_timeout`.

| Build | Processes | GGPO sessions | `end_match` sent | `game_closed` received | `match_teardown_timeout` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v0.9.8 (`56e26d9f`) | 3 | 31 | 29 | 29 | 0 |
| rc builds (22 to 23 Sep) | 5 | 6 | 2 | 0 | 5 |

In the v0.9.8 processes every `end_match` was answered by `game_closed` within 7 to 27 ms. The two sessions without an `end_match` are the two processes that died mid-match (F-015). The rc logs show the failure exactly as [2026-09-24-wine-dxvk-check](2026-09-24-wine-dxvk-check.md) described it: `end_match` sent, no answer, `helper load ... last_event=sent last_event_age_ms=30126`, then the room closed. Fixed by 7b549ad.

One residual: on the rc builds the player first read "Your Ready did not go through. The room did not finish the previous match in time" (the parked Ready's 20 s budget) and 10 s later the teardown message. The Ready budget is now derived from the helper teardown bound, so the message that explains the failure comes first.

## F-015: two processes died silently after 12 and 15 matches

Reported as: after about ten games the lobby freezes in the middle of a game and the game closes for one or both players, on Windows and Linux, with nothing in the log.

| Process | Started | Matches | Last line | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pid 484 (`sf4e.1.log`) | 24 Sep 23:25 | 12 | `NetplayFacade GGPO phase 3 -> 4` (match 12, 0 s after Running) | 23:58:24 |
| pid 792 (`sf4e.log`) | 24 Sep 23:58 | 15 | `GGPO: timesync recommends 3 frames; outstanding pacing 0.0 ms` (match 15, 50 s in) | 25 Sep 00:38:35 |
| the peer of pid 792 | | 12 | seen from pid 792: `CONNECTION_INTERRUPTED`, then `DISCONNECTED_FROM_PEER` at 00:29:52, room recovered | |

Both local processes and the peer's died with an ordinary last line and no warning. The helper status logs stop at the same moment with the match in phase 4 and the native socket live. Nothing recorded the fault, because nothing could:

- the sidecar had no exception filter, so an access violation or a fail-fast ended the process without a line;
- GGPO's assertion macro showed a message box and called `exit(1)`; its text went only to GGPO's own log, which ships disabled;
- the launcher read the game's exit code and did not log it.

What the logs rule out: no `reclaiming leaked slot` (the save-state pool is not leaking), the per-slot key counts alternate 89 and 90 by matchup and do not climb (`fKey::trackedKeys` is not accumulating), no `Match session failed`, no room error before the death. The timesync line is the last line by coincidence: it fires every 240 frames and its handler only stores a number.

What changed so the next crash names itself:

- `sf4e/logs/sf4e-crash.log` is written synchronously from the failing thread with the exception code, the faulting address as module plus offset, the access that failed and the last 64 log lines, and `sf4e-crash.dmp` beside it.
- A GGPO assertion writes the same record with its text (`kind=ggpo_assertion`) before GGPO exits.
- `launcher.log` gets `Game exited with code 0x... (name)`.
- `sf4e.log` gets one `Process [start_ggpo]` and one `Process [battle_close]` line per match: private bytes, working set, the largest free region of the 32-bit address space, free regions, handles, threads, dropped log lines, and whether the crash filter is still ours.

Next measurement: one session of fifteen or more matches on a build with these changes. If it dies, send the whole `sf4e\logs` folder and `launcher.log`. The `Process [...]` lines over the session show whether anything grows; the crash record says where it died. Both players' folders are wanted, since the peer died in the same match.

## F-016: controlling both characters after a disconnect (fixed)

Reported with a video: the opponent's game "freaked out" and returned to the main menu, and the reporter then controlled both characters while the match continued locally. A tester added that it happens "when the match doesn't synchronize".

Root cause, from the code: an online match is a native offline Versus whose pad reads are replaced only while a GGPO session exists. Retiring the session reopened the update gate, and every path that retired the session mid-fight without also asking the battle to leave (closing the room, leaving or replacing it, a room lost while GGPO was still synchronizing) ran the native battle with the local controller on both slots. The fix claims every netplay battle before its session starts and drives a battle that has lost its session out with neutral input, re-asserting the exit every update until the engine closes it. The log line is `Netplay battle lost its session; leaving it`; `Netplay battle still alive 300 updates after losing its session` would mean the engine ignored the exit and is the line to report.

Owed: two-PC runs (leave the room mid-fight, kill the helper mid-fight, block UDP during "Synchronizing", kill the peer's game), each ending with both games at the menu and neither responding to the local pad in between.

## F-017: the lobby closes when someone joins during a fight

Reported as: when two people are in a match and a third joins the room, the room closes for the players. No log came with the report.

No authority path ends a match on a join; a join changes only the room revision. What a join does do is change the Raft voter set and push a checkpoint to every member, and three paths could turn that into a closed room: a helper error the room did not recognise closed the whole room, including two errors that concern one match (`stale_match`, `invalid_game_registration`); an out-of-order control message id failed the room; and a full helper event queue ended the helper actor and every gameplay link. The first and third are fixed; the second is logged and kept as the next candidate. `CustomRoomGameTest --late-join` admits a member in the middle of game one and requires every room to stay Ready.

Next measurement: the reporter's `sf4e.log` and `launcher.log` from all three members of one such room, right after it happens. Lines to search for:

- `Room: failed`
- `Room: helper error ... scope=`
- `Room: control message rejected`
- `Room: closing error=`
- `Room: event ... closes the room`
- `Match session failed:`
- `Helper: actor ... stuck in`
- `Game exited with code`

## F-018: desync when the two players chose different delays (not a delay mismatch)

Reported with the suggestion that seat one should decide the delay. Since v0.9.8-rc4 the authority computes one match delay for both seats, the higher of the two Ready choices (`RoomModel.hxx`, `MatchDelay`), every client freezes that projection for the generation, and each side applies the value for its own seat. GGPO's frame delay is local to each side: it only shifts when that side's inputs are stamped, so two different values cannot desynchronise the simulation, and `IrohAuthorizedMatchTest` plays every cycle with different per-seat delays and checks both input streams. Letting seat one decide would change nothing.

The logs now show `Netplay: match delay seats=a/b local_slot=n applied=d` at every start, and a refused `ggpo_set_frame_delay` is a warning. To look further, both players' `sf4e.log` from one bad match are needed: matching `seats=` values rule this out, and `Desync detected` or a battle-hash mismatch would point at the real cause. Rooms refuse mixed builds, so an rc3 or older room is not a case of this.
